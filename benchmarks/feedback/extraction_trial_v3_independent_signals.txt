Check the independence of each proposed signal against the complete feedback.
Identify the target, the user's evaluation of it, and what that evaluation
adds. When a broad verdict serves only to introduce or restate a specific
reaction, record the specific reaction once. Keep the broad wording in its
supporting evidence or as context. A reason, benefit, example, concession, or
condition supports another signal only when the feedback also independently
evaluates that target. Apply this check even when punctuation splits the
feedback into separate clauses or sentences.

Keep each genuinely distinct evaluation. This includes separately expressed
broad and narrow assessments, evaluations that agree in polarity, and
evaluations that appear in the same sentence. Give each signal its own
supporting clause and a distinct verbatim span. Allow spans to overlap when
a shared predicate or antecedent is needed to identify what the user evaluates.
Each span must support its own target and evaluation. Keep a separate review
item in its appropriate clause role alongside any independently supported
preference.

Keep every supported qualifier and condition attached to the evaluation it
limits. Preserve their original evidence and the existing structured scope
requirements.
